The Card for Your Accountant

Eight questions fill one printable card. The card lists what a buyer will dig into first, then which of three paths suits you and why. Selling takes months of preparation. Print the card and take it to your accountant. That meeting is where preparation starts.

Prepare the data room

A buyer asks for three years of books and every key contract before any price is fixed. An AI tool on your own computer can sort them. A person still checks every line.

  • Three years of accounts, bank statements and tax returns
  • Customer contracts, with change-of-control clauses flagged
  • Supplier agreements, staff contracts, lease and licences

What a person still reviews

  • Your accountant reconciles each year to the bank and signs the sorted list.
  • You decide which contracts go in and which wait for a signed confidentiality agreement.
  • A lawyer reads every consent and change-of-control clause the AI flags.

Eight questions

About three minutes. The card updates as you answer. Each question is one a buyer asks. Each one is tagged with the assumption it tests.

Path 1. Full sale.
Sell all of it to an outside buyer.
Path 2. Sell over time.
Staff or family buy it in instalments paid from profits.
Path 3. Sell a majority, keep a share.
Sell most of it now. Sell the rest later.

Figures you set

How the card is worked out

Path score   = sum of the points each answer gives that path (shown below each run)
Second run   = the two weakest buyer-checked answers (questions 1–5) set to their weakest option, then scored again
Path 3 rule  = offered only if it is on top in both runs
Prep months  = 3 + one month for each step an answer sits below the strongest option (questions 1–5)

Base price   = annual profit × multiple
Bad case     = base price × (1 − price cut) × share you actually receive
Good case    = base price × (1 + uplift), path 3 kept share × (1 + growth)
Share received
  Path 1 = 100%
  Path 2 = up front + (1 − up front) × (years paid ÷ term)
  Path 3 = share sold + (1 − share sold) × kept-share factor
Kept-share factor by "who runs it": manager 1.0, staff cope 0.8, slows down 0.5, stops 0.2
Holds        = bad case ≥ the lowest total you would accept
Room to push = 1 − floor ÷ (base price × share received in bad case)

AI prep      = hours × accountant rate
Fees lost    = deal fees − (hours without AI − hours with AI) × rate

Five assumptions in the brief, checked

  • You know three paths exist. Most owners do not, so they are named above.
  • "Suits the buyer" means the other side of the table. This card says which path suits you.
  • Eight questions are enough if each tests one thing a buyer checks.
  • You want a price. Not assumed. The price band sits last on the card.
  • You want to sell all of it. Not assumed. Question 6 tests it.
