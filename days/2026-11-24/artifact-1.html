The Repayment Schedule

Borrower files that arrive as scanned PDFs and three versions of one spreadsheet cost analyst hours. This page prices those hours. It starts with the files that die before committee. It then shows the month an automated step repays its setup. Review hours by a named analyst come off before any figure is shown.

Bad, base and good case

Cost with the step = remaining analyst hours × hourly cost × weeks + share or fee. Setup is counted in payback. Review hours stay fixed when volume falls.

Fixed fee or share of measured savings

Same inputs, two ways to pay for the step. The fixed fee is the usual path. The share is the shortcut the team chose as the default, because its cost falls when volume falls.

What the step does, and what a person still reviews

The automated step

  • Reads scanned statements and spreadsheets and lays the figures into your template.
  • Flags duplicate versions of the same file and missing periods.
  • Drafts the list of documents to chase from the borrower.

A named analyst still reviews

  • Every cleaned file before it enters the credit model. This is the review time already subtracted above.
  • Covenant definitions, and any reconciliation that changes a ratio.
  • The decision to send a file to committee or drop it.

Formulas

H = weekly cleanup hours, c = hourly cost, w = weeks a year, d = share of files that die,
a = share automated, r = review hours a week, s = share of savings, F = fixed fee a month, S = setup hours

Without the step, per year = H × c × w
Dead-file cost, per year = H × c × w × d
Hours left with the step, weekly = H − a×H + r
Net hours returned, weekly = a×H − r
Gross saving, per year = (a×H − r) × c × w
Share path net = gross × (1 − s)
Fee path net = gross − 12 × F
Payback month = first month where cumulative net ≥ S × c
Share path stays positive while H > r ÷ a
Fee path breaks even at H = (12×F ÷ (c×w) + r) ÷ a
