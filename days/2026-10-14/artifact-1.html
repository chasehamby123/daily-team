Your pipeline

One deal per line. Fields: deal | stage | days in stage | EV | equity needed | equity committed | sunk costs | days left on exclusivity. Separate with | or tab. Write money as 18M or 140k. Stages: LOI, diligence, equity raise, debt, close. List a deal twice if it sits in two stages. The log keeps the worse one.

Normal days per stage: LOI 30, diligence 60, equity raise 45, debt 45, close 30. Past normal counts as stalled.

Your terms. These come from your own agreements and may depend on local rules. Enter yours.

Three cases

Good case: usual raise 30 days faster, partner 9 days faster, committed equity up 20% (capped at equity needed), exit multiple up 0.5. The slip lengthens the usual raise only. Partner delay is entered on its own line.

The way around

Fifteen family offices, one at a time, is the usual raise. The way around is one capital partner who fills the gap in one approval cycle. The terms are written out so you can put them on a napkin.

Usual raise
  • Equity gap filled piece by piece.
  • Days: the usual raise figure above.
  • If exclusivity lapses, you carry the sunk costs.
  • If it closes, you keep the full fee and promote.
Capital partner
  • One check fills the gap, up to the partner's largest check.
  • Partner takes the share of promote entered above. Default 30%.
  • Board control stays with you.
  • Partner pays the dead costs if the deal fails after they approve it.

Both routes use the same inputs from your pipeline line. The results sit side by side in each stalled row and in the three cases.

AI first read

Before you commission the QoE engagement, a model reads the CIM and the QoE draft. It pulls the figures, lists add-backs, and flags where the two documents disagree. A named CPA reviews and signs off. The QoE engagement costs the same on both paths. The first read tells you whether to commission it.

A person still reviews:

  • The CPA signs off on revenue quality, add-backs, and the working capital peg. Their name goes on the read.
  • You read customer concentration and the top contracts yourself.
  • Your counsel reviews legal terms. The model output is not legal advice.

Bad case for the AI path: the documents are messy, the model misreads add-backs, and the CPA redoes more of the work.

Formulas

Fee = fee % × EV Promote = promote % × max(0, equity × (multiple − 1) − equity × ((1 + pref)^years − 1)) Yours at risk = fee + promote + sunk costs Per day = sum over stalled deals of (yours at risk ÷ days left on exclusivity) Gap = equity needed − equity committed Usual route = closes if usual raise days ≤ exclusivity days left closes: fee + promote lapses: − sunk costs Partner route = closes if gap ≤ partner check and approval days ≤ exclusivity days left closes: fee + promote × (1 − partner share) lapses: − sunk costs AI path cost = AI read + CPA hours × CPA rate (days = 1 + CPA hours ÷ 8) Analyst cost = analyst hours × analyst rate (days = analyst hours ÷ 8)

Days in stage and exclusivity are counted in calendar days. Days are rounded up.
